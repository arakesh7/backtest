01 BarHistory Slicing vs. Array Indexing Trap (Inverted TA-Lib Arrays & closes[-1] Misconception)
src/backtester/data/history.py:38-40 • strategies/user_strategies.py:626-628
What is happening (Plain English)

Direct integer indexing works as intended: When querying BarHistory directly with integers, the custom recency logic functions correctly: bh[0] gives the current bar (latest), bh[-1] gives the previous bar (1 bar earlier), and bh[-2] gives 2 bars earlier.

The critical bug occurs during SLICING: When a strategy requests an array slice using bars = self.bar_history[symbol][:], BarHistory.__getitem__(slice) returns a NumPy array ordered from newest to oldest: [current, 1_bar_ago, 2_bars_ago, ..., oldest_bar]. This creates two catastrophic failure points:

  • 1. The closes[-1] Tail-Indexing Trap: On a standard Python list or NumPy array, arr[-1] accesses the last element of the array. Because the slice places the oldest bar at the tail of the array, writing closes = np.array(bars); current_close = closes[-1] (as in RsiMeanReversionStrategy) does not get the previous bar—it extracts the oldest bar from 200 bars ago! The author confused BarHistory's recency indexing (where bh[-1] is 1 bar ago) with NumPy array tail indexing (where closes[-1] is the oldest element).
  • 2. TA-Lib Backwards Computation: Libraries like TA-Lib expect strictly chronological data (Index 0 = past, Index -1 = present). Because bars[:] feeds data backwards, TA-Lib computes moving averages and RSI in reverse time. Taking fast_ma[-1] evaluates the moving average of the oldest bars from 200 bars ago, not the current bars!
Backtest Illusion

Stock price rallied from ₹100 to ₹140 over 20 bars. The buffer slice yields [140, 138, ..., 100]. The strategy calls current_close = closes[-1] and inspects ₹100 instead of ₹140. TA-Lib computes on this reversed list, and fast_ma[-1] evaluates the oldest historical bars (₹100 to ₹120). The strategy believes the stock is oversold at ₹100 and buys at the exact peak of ₹140.

Live Trading Reality

In live trading, TA-Lib receives live chronological streams where the latest price is ₹140, not ₹100. The live strategy fires completely different signals at completely different times. The profitable backtest curve was a mathematical illusion created by trading on reversed historical noise.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix:
  1. Keep direct integer indexing intact: bh[0] remains current bar, bh[-1] remains 1 bar earlier.
  2. In BarHistory.__getitem__(slice), change the slice generation sequence so that bh[:] returns in strictly chronological order (oldest to newest): starting at (self.head - self.count + 1) and stepping forward to self.head.
  3. This ensures that when a NumPy array is passed to TA-Lib or sliced, closes[-1] is the current bar, closes[0] is the oldest bar, and all rolling indicators compute in standard forward chronological order.
02 Polarity Trajectory Fallacy Awards Free Profits on Market Crash Days
src/backtester/execution/broker.py:164-190
What is happening (Plain English)

When a stock's High reaches your profit target and its Low reaches your stop-loss within the same bar, the broker must decide which one was hit first. The default POLARITY model assumes that if a candle closed red (Close < Open), the price path must have been Open -> High -> Low -> Close. This means on any huge selloff bar that opened, ticked up slightly, and collapsed, the broker assumes the High was touched first, automatically granting you a full take-profit win before the crash occurred.

Backtest Illusion

You hold 1,000 shares of Tata Motors at ₹1,000. Stop-Loss is ₹980 (-2%), Target is ₹1,010 (+1%). On bad earnings news, the stock opens at ₹1,000, spikes briefly to ₹1,011, and crashes to ₹920 (Close ₹930). Because Close < Open, the broker assumes the target was hit first. Reported PnL: +₹10,000 Profit.

Live Trading Reality

In reality, panic selling hit the open immediately. The price plunged directly through ₹980, triggering the stop-loss with slippage at ₹975. The brief uptick happened hours later during late-session short covering. Actual PnL: -₹25,000 Loss. A ₹35,000 divergence on a single trade.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Change the default execution model from POLARITY to CONSERVATIVE. In conservative mode, whenever a bar touches both the target and the stop-loss, always assume the stop-loss was hit first. This enforces institutional risk standards: a backtest should never give itself the benefit of the doubt on ambiguous intrabar data.
03 Broker.equity Evaluates Cost Basis, Not Mark-to-Market Value
src/backtester/execution/broker.py:40 • sizer.py:57
What is happening (Plain English)

Broker.equity is calculated as cash + sum(pos.size * pos.avg_price). However, avg_price is fixed at the original purchase price (cost basis). When you buy shares, your cash decreases by the cost, and your position increases by the exact same cost. Because the formula uses entry price rather than current market price, equity remains permanently identical to initial cash while positions are open. If your holdings drop 50% or double, Broker.equity does not move.

Backtest Illusion

Starting with ₹10 Lakhs, you invest ₹10 Lakhs in stock. The market enters a severe 40% correction; actual portfolio value drops to ₹6 Lakhs. Broker.equity still calculates: Cash (0) + (Shares * Entry Price) = ₹10 Lakhs. The position sizer risks 2% of equity (₹20,000) on the next trade, believing capital is intact.

Live Trading Reality

Risking ₹20,000 on a true account value of ₹6 Lakhs is actually 3.33% risk per trade. The strategy aggressively over-leverages during severe drawdowns instead of scaling down size. A series of losses compounds into account wipeout.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Mark positions to market on every bar. Update Broker.equity to calculate: self.cash + sum(pos.size * current_bar_close). When prices fall, equity must decrease so sizers automatically scale down risk; when prices rise, equity must increase to allow compounding.
04 Realized Trade PnL Ignores Commissions & Commission Model Decoupled
src/backtester/execution/broker.py:350 • analyzer.py:90
What is happening (Plain English)

When an exit order fills, _calculate_realized_pnl computes profit as (exit_price - entry_price) * size. It does not deduct commissions or exchange fees. The Trade object records 100% gross profit. The analyzer then takes every trade with PnL > 0 and counts it as a winning trade. Furthermore, the Commission classes in commission.py are completely disconnected from the Broker class, leaving Broker.commission = 0 by default.

Backtest Illusion

An intraday scalping strategy executes 1,000 trades per year, making an average gross gain of ₹15 per trade. Total transaction costs (brokerage, STT, exchange turnover fees, GST) are ₹35 per round trip. The backtest reports: 1,000 trades, 70% win rate, and +₹15,000 net profit.

Live Trading Reality

In live trading, you paid ₹35,000 in transaction costs. Your actual net result is: ₹15,000 - ₹35,000 = -₹20,000 Loss. A losing strategy that burns money on broker fees appears highly profitable in the audit report.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Inject an active commission model into Broker. In _calculate_realized_pnl, deduct the entry commission and exit commission from the realized PnL before creating the Trade object. All downstream metrics (win rate, profit factor, average win/loss) must reflect net, post-cost reality.

05 Short Liabilities Anchored to Entry Price (Infinite Insolvency Leverage)
src/backtester/execution/broker.py:48
What is happening (Plain English)

When you short-sell, the broker reserves cash to guarantee you can buy the shares back. In get_available_cash(), the reserved cash is calculated as abs(size) * entry_price * 1.5. If the shorted stock quadruples in price, your real liability has skyrocketed, but the broker still calculates your required reserve based on the cheap initial entry price.

Backtest Illusion

You short 1,000 shares of BSE at ₹100 with ₹2 Lakhs cash. Cash becomes ₹3 Lakhs; reserve is ₹1.5 Lakhs. BSE surges 4x to ₹400 in a massive squeeze. Your real short loss is -₹3 Lakhs (net account equity is -₹1 Lakh bankrupt). The broker still calculates reserve at ₹100, reports ₹1.5 Lakhs in "free available cash", and allows opening more trades.

Live Trading Reality

In real trading, the broker issues a margin call when price passes ₹150. At ₹200, the broker forcefully liquidates the account to protect against default. The account is dead, yet the backtest shows it continuing to trade and recover.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Mark short liabilities to market using the current bar's closing price: short_reserve = sum(abs(pos.size) * current_price * (1.0 + margin_ratio)). If required reserve exceeds total cash, set available cash to 0 and halt new positions.
06 STOPLIMIT Orders Fail to Fill Across Subsequent Bars
src/backtester/execution/broker.py:379-389
What is happening (Plain English)

A Stop-Limit order has a trigger price and a limit price. Once price touches the trigger, the order should become a regular limit order that remains active until filled. In broker.py, the code requires both the trigger and the limit to be satisfied on the exact same bar. If it triggers on Bar 1 but cannot fill, on Bar 2 it requires the trigger price to be breached again.

Backtest Illusion

Buy Stop-Limit with Trigger ₹500, Limit ₹502. Bar 1 triggers at ₹501, but closes at ₹500.50 without filling at the limit. On Bar 2, the stock opens at ₹501.50 and rallies to ₹510. The order should have filled at ₹501.50. Instead, because Bar 2 did not breach ₹500 from below, the order is ignored and never fills.

Live Trading Reality

On live exchanges, once triggered, the order sits on the limit book. It fills immediately on Bar 2 at ₹501.50. The backtest misses a multi-day winner that the live strategy actually captured.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Add an is_triggered = True flag to the Order object. Once the trigger price is breached, permanently set is_triggered = True and evaluate the order as a standard LIMIT order on all subsequent bars until filled or canceled.
07 Phantom Strategy Stop-Loss Orders (Never Submitted to Broker)
strategies/user_strategies.py:132-137 • strategy/base.py
What is happening (Plain English)

In strategies like OHLCReversalStrategy, the code calls self.buy(..., stop_loss_price=current_open * 0.98). In Strategy.buy(), this stop price is passed only to the position sizer to calculate share size. No stop-loss order is ever created in the broker, and the strategy has no code in on_data to monitor the price. The position trades completely unhedged.

Backtest Illusion

Strategy documentation promises: "Risk Management: 2% Stop Loss". The backtest author assumes every trade has downside strictly capped at 2% by the broker.

Live Trading Reality

During a market breakdown, the stock plummets 15%. Because no stop order exists in the broker and the strategy doesn't monitor the price, the position remains open all day until the 15:25 EOD close, suffering an unhedged -15% loss.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: When Strategy.buy() or sell() receives a stop_loss_price, automatically submit a linked OCO stop order to the Broker so the broker's matching engine enforces the stop loss directly on every bar.
08 Unreserved Capital on Multi-Order Bursts (Random Allocation by Dict Iteration)
src/backtester/execution/broker.py:248-311
What is happening (Plain English)

When a multi-stock strategy generates 5 buy orders on the same bar, each order checks purchasing power against current cash. Because none of the orders have executed yet, all 5 pass the check. On the next bar, the broker processes orders one by one. The first order consumes the cash; the remaining orders are rejected. Because Python dictionary keys are iterated arbitrarily, which stock gets bought depends purely on random hashing luck.

Backtest Illusion

You have ₹1 Lakh cash. Strategy ranks Stock A as #1 (Score 9.8) and Stock E as #5 (Score 4.2). Both need ₹60,000. Due to dictionary hashing, Stock E is processed first and fills. Stock A is rejected with Insufficient available cash. The backtest runs with the worst stock instead of the best stock.

Live Trading Reality

In institutional execution, capital is strictly allocated according to strategy ranking score. The backtest's performance is dominated by dictionary sorting luck rather than the strategy's predictive edge.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Implement pre-trade capital reservation. When an order is placed, reserve its estimated capital in an unreserved_cash pool so subsequent orders in the same bar size against remaining funds. Alternatively, sort pending orders by priority/score before processing.
09 Silent Order Rejection & Strategy State Desynchronization
src/backtester/execution/broker.py:258 • strategy/base.py
What is happening (Plain English)

When strategy.buy() places an order, it receives an Order in CREATED status. On the next bar, if the broker rejects the order due to lack of margin, the strategy is never notified. The strategy assumes it owns the position, marks trade_taken = True, and starts managing trailing stops for a ghost position.

Backtest Illusion

An order for Reliance is rejected by the broker. The strategy continues logging: "[2024-01-02] RELIANCE: Trailing stop raised to ₹2,550". Because trade_taken = True, it ignores real buy signals for the rest of the week.

Live Trading Reality

The strategy's internal state machine is completely broken. It manages phantom risk on a non-existent trade and locks itself out of taking legitimate winning trades.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Add an order rejection callback: strategy.on_order_rejected(order, reason). When the broker rejects an order, notify the strategy immediately so it can clear its flags and reset its internal state.

10 Intraday Sharpe Ratio Compressed by 19.4x on 1-Minute Data
src/backtester/analytics/analyzer.py:105-111
What is happening (Plain English)

In DefaultAnalyzer, the Sharpe ratio is calculated by multiplying average return over standard deviation by np.sqrt(252). This formula is only correct for daily returns. On 1-minute data, there are 375 bars per day, so 94,500 bars per year. The correct multiplier is $\sqrt{94,500} \approx 307.4$. Multiplying by 15.87 instead of 307.4 understates the Sharpe ratio by a factor of $\sqrt{375} \approx 19.36\times$.

Backtest Illusion

An exceptional intraday momentum strategy achieves a true annualized Sharpe ratio of 2.50. The backtester prints: Sharpe Ratio (Annualized): 0.13. The researcher concludes the strategy has virtually zero risk-adjusted return and discards it.

Live Trading Reality

The strategy is actually an institutional-grade alpha producer, but the backtester's hardcoded daily annualization formula compressed the metric by ~20x.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Dynamically compute the annualization factor based on the backtester frequency: factor = $\sqrt{\text{bars\_per\_day} \times 252}$. For 1-minute bars, use $\sqrt{375 \times 252} \approx 307.4$. Alternatively, resample the equity curve to daily closes before calculating Sharpe.
11 Breakeven Trades Dropped from Closed Trades (Synthetic 100% Win Rates)
src/backtester/analytics/analyzer.py:122-146
What is happening (Plain English)

The analyzer filters completed trades using: closing_trades = [t for t in self.trades if t.pnl != 0]. Any trade that exited at breakeven (PnL = 0) is discarded. It then computes win rate as winning_trades / len(closing_trades).

Backtest Illusion

A strategy executes 10 trades: 2 winning trades (+₹100 each), 0 losing trades, and 8 trades scratched at ₹0 breakeven. The analyzer drops the 8 breakeven trades and calculates: 2 wins / 2 counted trades = 100% Win Rate!

Live Trading Reality

True gross win rate is 2 / 10 = 20%. Furthermore, after transaction fees of ₹20 per trade, those 8 breakeven trades were actually net losses. The strategy has an 80% loss rate in reality.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Count all closed round-trip trades in the denominator, regardless of whether PnL was positive, negative, or zero: win_rate = len(winning_trades) / total_completed_trades.
12 Missing Bars & Exchange Holidays Cause Flash -100% Drawdowns
src/backtester/analytics/analyzer.py:64-70
What is happening (Plain English)

On each bar, the analyzer calculates position value using: sum(pos.size * data[pos.asset][4] for pos in broker.positions.values() if pos.asset in data). If a single 1-minute bar is missing from the CSV, or on an exchange holiday, pos.asset in data is False. The analyzer values your holdings at ₹0 for that minute.

Backtest Illusion

You hold ₹5 Lakhs of shares and ₹0 cash. At 11:30 AM, one quote is missing from the CSV. The analyzer records portfolio value as ₹0. On the next bar, it jumps back to ₹5 Lakhs. The final report permanently records: "Max Drawdown (%)": -100.0%.

Live Trading Reality

In live trading, your positions are safely held with the broker. The reported 100% wipeout was a synthetic artifact caused by the analyzer failing to forward-fill the last known price.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Implement mark-to-market forward-filling. If an asset is missing from the current bar's market data, value the position using its last known closing price rather than treating it as zero.
13 Uncapped Position Sizing When Available Funds Are Negative
src/backtester/orders/sizer.py:88-95
What is happening (Plain English)

In FixedRiskPercentSizer.get_size(), the affordability safety check states: if available_funds > 0: raw_size = min(raw_size, max_affordable). If available funds are zero or negative (e.g. short liabilities exceed cash), the if block is skipped completely, and the function returns raw_size without any cap!

Backtest Illusion

An account has -₹15,000 available cash due to margin requirements. The strategy generates a buy signal with a tight stop (Risk/share = ₹5, Capital to risk = ₹20,000). The formula computes 4,000 shares (₹40 Lakhs notional). Because funds are not > 0, the cap is skipped and the sizer approves the 4,000-share order.

Live Trading Reality

A position sizer should never approve trades when purchasing power is non-existent. Bypassing the cap allows massive orders to be sent to the broker while over-leveraged.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Add an immediate guard clause at the start of get_size(): if available_funds <= 0: return 0.
14 Ignored Risk-Free Rate in Sharpe Ratio Calculation
src/backtester/analytics/analyzer.py:107
What is happening (Plain English)

DefaultAnalyzer.__init__ accepts a risk_free_rate argument. However, in get_results(), the formula simply divides mean return by standard deviation without subtracting the hurdle rate.

Backtest Illusion

A strategy returns 7.5% annually in a regime where Indian Treasury Bills yield 7.0% risk-free. The backtest reports a healthy Sharpe ratio of 0.85.

Live Trading Reality

Excess return over risk-free cash is only 0.5%. The true excess Sharpe ratio is only 0.06. The strategy takes equity market risk to deliver zero economic alpha over a risk-free bank deposit.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Convert the annual risk-free rate to a per-bar rate, subtract it from each bar's return, and calculate Sharpe on the resulting excess returns series.

15 Blind UTC Localization Shifts Local IST Data by +5:30 Hours
src/backtester/data/loader.py:26-31
What is happening (Plain English)

In CSVDataLoader.load(), if timestamps lack timezone info, the code assumes they are UTC and converts them to Asia/Kolkata. But Indian data feeds (Zerodha, TrueData, Upstox) already store timestamps in IST! Converting them adds an extra 5 hours and 30 minutes to all timestamps.

Backtest Illusion

A 09:15:00 market opening bar becomes 14:45:00. The trading calendar ends the day at 15:30:00. The backtester only matches 45 minutes of trading data per day (14:45 to 15:30), discarding 5 hours and 15 minutes of market data.

Live Trading Reality

Live trading operates across all 375 minutes of the session, encountering opening volatility that was completely absent from the backtest.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Add a parameter source_tz=None. If the input data is already in local exchange time (IST), do not apply timezone conversions.

19 End-of-Day (EOD) Square-Off Lag & Daily Frequency Blindspot
src/backtester/strategy/base.py:46-56
What is happening (Plain English)

close_on_eod checks if ts.time() == self.market_close_time (15:25:00). On daily backtests (freq="D"), timestamps are 00:00:00, so this check is never true. On 1-minute backtests, orders placed at 15:25 execute on the next bar at 15:26.

Backtest Illusion

An intraday strategy is tested on daily bars. Because ts.time() == 15:25:00 is never met, positions are held overnight across multi-day swings, appearing as a multi-day swing strategy.

Live Trading Reality

In live trading, intraday MIS positions are forcefully squared off by the broker at 15:20. Live performance bears no relation to the backtest.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic

20 BarHistory Boundary Wrap-Around on get(-self.count)
src/backtester/data/history.py:16-26
What is happening (Plain English)

In BarHistory.get(idx), the guard check is if idx < -self.count: raise IndexError. When the buffer is full (count == 120), calling get(-120) passes because -120 < -120 is False. The modulo math (head - 120) % 120 evaluates to head, silently returning the newest bar instead of the oldest!

Backtest Illusion

A lookback indicator queries get(-120) to inspect the price 120 bars ago. It gets the current price instead, dividing (current - current) = 0, suppressing momentum signals.

Live Trading Reality

The calculation is mathematically corrupted due to circular buffer wrap-around.

Required Fix & Plain English Logic
The Fix: Update the index guard: if idx < -self.count + 1: raise IndexError so querying beyond the valid window raises an error rather than wrapping to the head.
